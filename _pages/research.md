## Job Market Paper

### The Effects of Pay Transparency on Mid- to Late-Career Workers

This paper studies the effects of Colorado's cross-firm pay transparency law, the Equal Pay for Equal Work Act (EPEWA), on the labor market outcomes of mid- to late-career workers aged 45–64. Using a difference-in-differences design, I find that pay transparency increases weekly earnings and hourly wages, with larger wage gains among women. The policy also increases employment and working hours in the short run and temporarily narrows the gender wage gap. Evidence on workers' beliefs shows that pay transparency raises expected wages from outside opportunities and reservation wages, while increasing perceived job-offer probabilities in the short run. Together, the findings suggest that reducing wage information frictions can improve mid- to late-career workers' evaluation of outside opportunities and strengthen their labor market outcomes.

## Working Papers

### The Effect of Public Long-Term Care Insurance in China on Adult Children's Labor Supply

This paper studies how China's public long-term care insurance (LTCI) affects adult children's labor supply using staggered city-level policy implementation and parental eligibility. I find that parental LTCI eligibility increases adult children's labor supply on the extensive margin, with larger effects among daughters. The results suggest that formal long-term care can affect not only care recipients but also the labor market behavior of family members by reducing informal caregiving responsibilities.

### The Effects of SNAP Work Requirements on Self-Employment

This paper examines how SNAP work requirements affect self-employment and SNAP participation among Able-Bodied Adults Without Dependents (ABAWDs). Using variation in SNAP work-requirement waivers, I find that work requirements have little effect on overall self-employment but reduce SNAP participation among self-employed ABAWDs and increase their working hours. The effects also differ by gender and across different phases of the business cycle.

### Leveraging Neural Networks, Instrumental Variables, and Genetic Algorithms for Optimal Classroom Assignment

*with Om Prakash Bedant, Zhenbang Jiao, and Lei Bill Wang*

**Revise and Resubmit, Journal of Applied Economics**

We develop a framework that combines neural networks, instrumental variables, and genetic algorithms to estimate peer effects and design classroom assignments. The efficiency-maximizing assignment increases average peer effects but generates more unequal educational outcomes, while an equity-adjusted assignment achieves smaller efficiency gains with substantially more equal outcomes.

## Works in Progress

### How Does Family Structure Influence Women's Bargaining Power in the Labor Market?

This project studies whether the effects of pay transparency on women's labor market outcomes vary with family structure, with particular attention to marriage and children.

### The Effect of Paid Family Leave on Aging People's Retirement and Saving

*with Linyan Hu*

This project studies how paid family and medical leave programs affect labor supply, retirement, and saving decisions among older individuals.

### The Effects of SNAP Work Requirements on Immigrants

This project studies how SNAP work-requirement waivers affect SNAP participation, employment, working hours, and food security among individuals in immigrant families.
